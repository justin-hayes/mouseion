# Experience direction

This direction document preserves the earlier Journey / Primary Goal
exploration. Those workflow-specific passages are historical, not current
product behavior; the shipped Reading workflow is defined in
[`../features/reading-workflow.md`](../features/reading-workflow.md).

The desired Mouseion product character is a **digital scholarly reading desk**.

This is not faux-historical styling or decorative academic theater. Mouseion
should feel:

- serious but approachable;
- scholarly without feeling institutional;
- centered on books, authors, editions, passages, vocabulary, and reading;
- information-dense when useful, but visually calm;
- typographically strong;
- restrained in color and ornament;
- respectful of bibliographic and linguistic evidence;
- open-ended without feeling unfinished.

## What should lead

Bibliographic identity and learner intention lead the hierarchy. Analysis is
preparation evidence around a book, not the visual subject of the product.

**My Books** should feel like a contemporary personal scholarly catalog,
capable of holding desired, distant, unassessed, stale, and currently active
works without making each one look like a task.

Historically, the design direction explored an ordered Reading Journey and
Primary Goal. The shipped Reading experience instead presents one current Book
or an unordered To Read chooser; it uses this document's calm, book-centered
visual principles without adopting that retired model.

Current and projected vocabulary effects may use restrained alignment,
continuity, or before-and-after comparison. The semantic reading order remains
a bibliographic list; spatial treatment never replaces exact text.

## Visual character

Use fine rules, clear whitespace, modest corners, and typography before adding
containers. Sans-serif typography is appropriate for application controls and
navigation. Serif typography may distinguish book titles, quotations, or source
text where it strengthens textual identity. Serif is not a generic scholarly
decoration.

On wide viewports, the single primary navigation occupies a quiet scholarly
margin beside the working leaf. Annotation blue identifies current context,
links, and focus; it is not a decorative wash. Catalog entries, Journey books,
evidence, and operations should read as distinct kinds of ruled records rather
than interchangeable cards. On compact viewports the margin folds into a top
index without changing document order.

Avoid defaulting to generic SaaS aesthetics such as:

- interchangeable dashboard cards;
- oversized metric tiles or headings;
- excessive rounded containers;
- decorative gradients or shadows;
- multiple competing navigation systems;
- status pills that collapse distinct facts;
- metrics without clear explanatory value;
- optimization-led hero content.

Also avoid fantasy maps, constellations, destination flags, quests,
achievements, XP, levels, progress trophies, or project-management imagery.
Mouseion's sense of continuation comes from books ahead, changed evidence, and
a fresh learner choice—not decoration.
