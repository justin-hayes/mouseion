# Latin batch 1: generator gate report

This is the gate report for the first Latin batch
([#2686](https://github.com/justin-hayes/mouseion/issues/2686), spec
[#2674](https://github.com/justin-hayes/mouseion/issues/2674)). It records
ADR 0132's four gates for each candidate Work, run on the real pinned archive.
It holds aggregates and IDs only. No source or translation text appears here.

Run on 2026-11-25. The inputs:

- **OLA:** v0.2.0, `opera_latina_adnotata_v0.2.0.zip` (Zenodo
  `10.5281/zenodo.15183688`, 3,857,914,226 bytes). Every byte the generator reads
  was fetched by HTTP Range request into a sparse local copy: the outer and inner
  zip directories and each Work's two members. Each member's CRC-32 matched its
  directory.
- **Translations:** `PerseusDL/canonical-latinLit@8cef5162`, the ADR 0133 pin.
- **Lewis & Short:** derived from `PerseusDL/lexica@56061ca1`
  `lat.ls.perseus-eng1.xml` with `nlp/scripts/derive_dictionary_index.py
  --ls-dir`.
- **Code:** the generator at `issue-2674-agile-lemur@83157a1e`, with the
  thresholds in `internal/classicalmanifest/gates.go`: split-word limit 2%, join
  floor 98%, tier B segment limit 400 words, dictionary floor 90%.

## Committed result

Twelve Latin Works are admitted. The committed manifest holds them with the 24
Greek definitions, generated by `make classical-manifest` from the real pins
(the sparse OLA archive, the `canonical-latinLit` translations and the derived
L&S index). `make verify-classical-manifest` reproduces it byte for byte without
the upstream archive.

- **Admitted on every gate:** *The Jugurthine War*, *Against Catiline*, *For
  Archias the Poet*, *In Defence of Milo*, *In Defence of Caelius*, *On
  Friendship* and *On Old Age*.
- **Admitted by Justin's clearance:** *The Gallic War*, *Agricola* and
  *Germania* (tier B segment limit), *The Conspiracy of Catiline* and *Life of
  Augustus* (join floor). See "Spot checks and clearances".
- **Dropped out:** *Life of Julius Caesar* (gate iv, not cleared). Its
  translation is removed from `layers.json` and `doc/third-party-data.md`.

Notes on the readings:

- *De Senectute*'s translation (`perseus-eng1`) marks section 35 with two
  section milestones, and the second carries the English of the Latin's
  section 36. The reader merges both into section 35; that is the reading
  measured above (orchestrator decision). Section 36 (5 sentences) is
  unjoined, and a card citing 35 shows the English of 35 and 36.
- *In Catilinam*'s translation is keyed speech.section, like the Latin, so it
  is tier A. Its override lists no contents: #2674 story 7 shows a Work
  divided into speeches with no contents list. Editorial textparts (`argument`, `intro`, `chronology`, `note`)
  are skipped in every translation.
- The tier B segment counts are the generator's (segment text, whitespace-split
  words).

## Reader defects

- **D5. Finer translation.** OLA cites the *Divus Iulius* by chapter only, but
  both translations are keyed chapter.section. A translation finer than the
  Latin is neither tier A nor tier B (ADR 0133), and the reader refuses it
  ("textpart deeper than citation depth"). It affects only *Life of Julius
  Caesar*, which has dropped out on gate (iv), so it is not being fixed for
  this batch.

## Tier eligibility

ADR 0133's tier B, a translation keyed one level above the Latin, allows
segments of at most 400 words. The generator now checks this in gate (iii)
(the "Tier" column above); a failure can be cleared by a reviewer note.
Three pinned translations are tier B and each has segments over the limit:
*The Gallic War* (10 of 404, largest 750), *Agricola* (1 of 46, 469) and
*Germania* (1 of 46, 460). No `perseus-engN` alternative at the pin is keyed
at the Latin's depth. Every other pinned translation is tier A.

Justin's clearances, recorded as each Work's `reviewerNote`:

- **Tier B segment limit (400 words).** The cost is passage length, not
  alignment; the tier A Works *Catiline* and *Life of Augustus* already show
  chapter-length passages of similar size.
  - *The Gallic War*: 10 of 404 segments over the limit, largest 750 words.
  - *Agricola*: 1 of 46, largest 469 (chapter 18).
  - *Germania*: 1 of 46, largest 460 (chapter 45).
- **Join floor (98%).** An unjoined sentence shows no English, never wrong
  English. The unjoined sentences are clustered, not scattered:
  - *The Conspiracy of Catiline* (681/698, 97.56%): 17 sentences, all in
    chapters 35 (11) and 57 (6). The translation keys neither chapter as a
    textpart; their English sits, after a page break, inside chapters 34 and
    56, so a card citing 34 or 56 shows the next chapter's English too.
  - *Life of Augustus* (732/761, 96.19%): 29 sentences, all in the last two
    chapters, 100 (15) and 101 (14), which the translation does not key.
    The translation numbers its chapters 1–99 and the Latin 1–101; at least at
    the end the numbering is shifted, since the translation's chapter 99 is
    the Latin's 101. Joined sentences near the end may therefore show another
    chapter's English. Where the shift begins has not been measured (see
    "Still to do").

## Deployment check

Run on 2026-11-25 against the committed manifest and layers, on a throwaway
stack: a Testcontainers PostgreSQL, the web handler and River workers in
process (wired as `cmd/server` wires them, with no NLP service and no
translation provider), and the derived L&S index. The check was a scratch
integration test kept outside the repository; it used none of the running
deployment's containers.

- **Catalog:** the Latin Catalog lists all 12 admitted Works.
- **Preparation:** adding *For Archias the Poet* fetched exactly its two
  archive members from Zenodo by Range (two `206` responses, 46,003 and 19,984
  bytes of 3,857,914,226) and the pinned translation from
  `canonical-latinLit@8cef5162`. The analysis published with 144 sentences and
  3,616 tokens, `syntax_state` `native_agdt`, and 32 translation segments.
- **Reading:** the Book started as the current reading. Sentence study and the
  Concordance show the Citations (for example 1, 18, 19, 25) and the stored
  Word relations, and no Construction.
- **Deck:** the prepared deck was ready with 168 cards and no provider call.
  Every card carries passage context from the pinned translation; 166 carry a
  Lewis & Short entry, 22 of them several stacked homograph entries (for
  example *sum*, three entries). The deck carries the OLA, `canonical-latinLit`
  and `PerseusDL/lexica` notices.

Result: **pass**.
